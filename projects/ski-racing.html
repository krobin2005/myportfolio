Ski Racing-Line Optimizer In progress

Can evolution find a faster line down a real World Cup Giant Slalom course than the world's best skiers? And can it beat gradient descent on the same problem?

RoleCo-builder with Liam Baron
WhenOct 2026 – Present
ToolsPython, JAX, evosax

Why evolution

In GS, a direct line is shorter but forces tight, slow turns, while a rounder line keeps your speed but covers more ground. You also can't miss a gate or crash. That makes for a bumpy problem with lots of rules and lots of local optima, which is where gradient descent tends to get stuck and evolution can do well.

How we built it

We built a skier simulator in Python and ran it on the real 3D terrain of the Gran Risa in Alta Badia, Italy, using the same course as the December 2025 men's World Cup GS. Each line is scored like a real race run, including the chance of crashing out. Then CMA-ES, a genetic algorithm we wrote ourselves, a hybrid that starts with CMA-ES and finishes with gradient descent, plain gradient descent and a grid search all tried to find the fastest line with the same amount of computing time.

Where it stands

This project is still in progress. So far, CMA-ES has found a line about a second and a half faster than the real winning run, and we made a race video where every algorithm's best line races head to head. Still to come: our reflections and GenAI disclosure, the class show and tell, and our final interviews in December.

Liam and I did the whole project together, every session and every prompt.
