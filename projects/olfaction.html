AI for Smell

A research lab run by software: AI agents that train neural networks to predict how molecules smell, get better with every run, and refuse to accept discoveries that don't hold up.

RoleLead of AI Research
WhenSep 2026 – Present
ToolsPython, PyTorch, scikit-learn, RDKit, Pyrfume

The question

Vision and hearing have well-established ways to measure what we sense, like wavelength and frequency. Smell doesn't have an equivalent. In Professor Jason Castro's lab at Bates, we're asking whether a neural network can learn to predict how a molecule smells (sweet, woody, fruity and so on) from its structure alone.

What I'm building

  • A six-agent research lab in Python that proposes ideas, trains neural networks on 5,858 molecules, and tests the results.
  • A Skeptic agent that checks every claimed improvement with scaffold splits, shuffled-label controls and corrected confidence intervals, so lucky results don't count as discoveries.
  • Memory across runs, so the lab builds on what it already learned.
  • Evolutionary search, where network designs breed and the best survive, bringing in what I'm learning in Evolutionary Computing.
  • A benchmark against the graph neural network approach of Lee et al. (Science, 2023).

Where it's headed

The paper is less about building the single best smell model and more about a lab that gets better at finding models with every run, while the Skeptic keeps false discoveries out. We're working toward a co-authored publication by the end of the fall 2026 semester.
