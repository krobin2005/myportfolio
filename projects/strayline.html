Strayline

A hybrid AI detector built to catch the network attacks nobody has seen before, and to show defenders exactly where it can and can't be trusted.

RoleFounder
WhenOct 2026 – Present
ToolsPython, PyTorch, CIC-IDS2017, UNSW-NB15

The problem

The cyberattack that hurts you most is the one nobody has seen before. Most AI security tools only recognize attacks they were trained on, and attackers now use AI to create new variants faster than defenders can label them. Analysts end up choosing between tools that miss new attacks and tools that bury them in false alarms, and detectors that look great in the lab often fail on real networks.

How it works

Strayline catches network traffic that strays from the normal line. It pairs two neural networks:

  • A classifier that learns the attack families we already know about.
  • An autoencoder trained only on normal traffic, so anything it can't reconstruct well gets flagged as unusual.
  • A hybrid rule: when the classifier isn't sure, the autoencoder gives a second opinion. Alert thresholds are set on normal traffic only, so the detector never sees the attack it's tested on.

The catchability map

The part I'm most excited about: I hide an entire attack family during training (DoS, port scans, brute-force logins, web attacks, botnets and more), then test whether the system still catches it. Repeating that for every family at a fixed 1% false-alarm rate produces a map of which unseen attacks are easy or hard to catch, and why. That tells security teams where they can trust the tool and where they still need a human.

Where it stands

  • Built the detector in PyTorch and ran the leave-one-family-out test on CIC-IDS2017: at a 1% false-alarm rate it catches 87% of never-seen infiltration traffic.
  • Added three Claude agents (triage, an investigator using an MCP tool server, and a literature researcher): 49,387 alerts became 414 incidents and 55 escalations, with all 8 real attack incidents escalated, including a never-seen port scan.
  • Submitted it as a research proposal to the AAAI-27 Undergraduate Consortium.
  • Selected as Bates College's runner-up for The Eleven Society's Northeast Entrepreneurship Summit (Boston, Nov 15, 2026).
  • Next: extend the catchability map to UNSW-NB15 and publish it.
